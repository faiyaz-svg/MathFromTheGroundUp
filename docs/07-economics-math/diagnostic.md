# Economics math diagnostic

This quiz covers the 22 lessons of the economics math folder, two questions on each, 44 in all. Take it without notes, and write every answer down before you look at the answers. A calculator is allowed, and you should use it where a question asks for a decimal. Most questions take one to three minutes, so plan on three or four sittings rather than one. Every question here is a small version of something its lesson teaches. Questions that use derivatives or Lagrange multipliers come from the [university core](../05-university-core/index.md).

Mark each answer right, wrong or guessed. A guess counts as wrong, even when it turns out right. Then do only the lessons behind the questions you marked wrong or guessed; the answer table names the lesson for each one. Several questions ask for a reason or a reading, and an answer without it counts as wrong. All data in the questions are made up (hypothetical). Money is in Canadian dollars.

## Measuring the economy

1\. A market has quantity demanded $Q = 120 - 4P$, where $P$ is the price in dollars. Find $Q$ at $P = 10$, then write the inverse demand $P$ as a function of $Q$ and give its intercept on the price axis.

2\. A straight demand curve passes through the points $(Q, P) = (20, 15)$ and $(60, 5)$. Write it as $P = a - bQ$.

3\. The unemployment rate rises from 5.0% to 6.0%. Give the change in percentage points and the percent change.

4\. A price rises 25% and then falls 25%. What is the net percent change?

5\. A country's output is 2000 in year 0, 2200 in year 1 and 2310 in year 2. Find the growth rate in each year and the compound annual growth rate over the two years.

6\. Output grows at a steady 3% a year. Find the exact doubling time, and compare it with the rule of 70.

7\. A price series is 40 in 2015, 46 in 2016 and 50 in 2017. (a) Write it as an index with 2015 = 100. (b) Rebase it so that 2016 = 100. (c) Find the percent change from 2016 to 2017.

8\. A basket has two goods. Base-year prices are \$2 and \$4 with quantities 10 and 5. Current prices are \$3 and \$4 with quantities 6 and 8. Find the Laspeyres and Paasche price indexes (base = 100).

9\. In a year, consumption is \$600, investment \$150, government purchases \$200, exports \$180 and imports \$170 (billions, hypothetical). Find GDP and net exports.

10\. A farmer sells wheat to a miller for \$30. The miller sells flour to a baker for \$50. The baker sells bread to consumers for \$90. Find each firm's value added and the amount these sales add to GDP.

11\. Nominal GDP is 1000 in year 0 and 1155 in year 1, and the GDP deflator is 100 in year 0 and 105 in year 1. Find real GDP in year 1 (in year 0 prices), nominal growth and real growth.

12\. In the base year, goods A and B sell for \$5 and \$10. This year 30 units of A and 20 of B are produced, at prices of \$6 and \$12. Find nominal GDP, real GDP in base-year prices and the deflator.

13\. The CPI is 130.0 in March of one year and 134.9 in March of the next. Find the inflation rate over those 12 months.

14\. The CPI is 118 now, with a base-year CPI of 100. A salary is \$59,000 now. Express it in base-year dollars. Then find the real wage growth when a nominal wage rises 4% and inflation is 2.5%.

15\. Of 1000 thousand people aged 15 and over, 600 thousand are employed, 50 thousand are unemployed and 350 thousand are not in the labour force. Find the unemployment rate, the participation rate and the employment rate.

16\. In question 15, 20 thousand of the unemployed become discouraged and stop looking for work. Find the new unemployment rate and participation rate, and say whether the labour market improved.

## Markets

17\. A country can make 60 phones or 30 cars with its resources, along a straight production possibilities frontier. Find the opportunity cost of one car in phones, and the number of phones it can make if it makes 10 cars.

18\. A bowed-out frontier is $y = 100 - x^2$. Find the marginal opportunity cost of $x$ in units of $y$ at $x = 3$ and at $x = 6$. Is the point $(4, 80)$ attainable, and is it efficient?

19\. In an hour, country A can make 6 shirts or 3 bags, and country B can make 4 shirts or 4 bags. Find each country's opportunity cost of a bag, and say who has the comparative advantage in each good.

20\. In question 19, the countries trade at 1.5 shirts per bag. Show that each gains from trading a bag, and give the range of prices at which both gain.

21\. Quantity demanded is $Q_d = 100 - 2P$ and quantity supplied is $Q_s = -20 + 4P$. Find the equilibrium price and quantity.

22\. In question 21, income rises and demand becomes $Q_d = 112 - 2P$. Find the new equilibrium. Is this a shift or a move along a curve?

23\. Use the market of question 21. Find consumer surplus and producer surplus at equilibrium.

24\. A tax of \$6 per unit is collected from sellers in the market of question 21. Find the price buyers pay, the price sellers receive, the tax revenue and the deadweight loss.

25\. For $Q = 100 - 2P$, find the price elasticity of demand at $P = 20$. Is demand elastic or inelastic there, and what happens to revenue if the price rises?

26\. When the price rises from \$10 to \$12, quantity demanded falls from 50 to 40. Find the midpoint (arc) elasticity and say whether demand is elastic.

## Firms and consumers

27\. A firm's cost is $C(q) = 200 + 10q + 0.5q^2$ dollars. Find the marginal cost at $q = 10$ from the derivative and compare it with the exact cost of the 11th unit.

28\. A firm's revenue is $R(q) = 100q - 2q^2$. Find marginal revenue, the output that maximizes revenue and the maximum revenue.

29\. With capital fixed, output is $Q = 4\sqrt{L}$, the wage is \$20 per unit of labour and fixed cost is \$100. Write the cost function $C(Q)$, then find marginal cost and average cost at $Q = 8$.

30\. Output is $Q = 5L^{0.5}K^{0.5}$. What are the returns to scale? Find the marginal product of labour at $L = 16$, $K = 25$.

31\. A price-taking firm faces a price of \$40 and has cost $C(q) = 50 + q^2$. Find its profit-maximizing output and its profit.

32\. A monopoly faces demand $P = 100 - Q$ and has constant marginal cost \$20 and no fixed cost. Find its output, price and profit, and the deadweight loss compared with a competitive market.

33\. A consumer has utility $U = xy$. Find the marginal rate of substitution at $(4, 8)$, and the value of $y$ on the indifference curve through $(4, 8)$ when $x = 8$.

34\. With $U = xy$, which is preferred, $(6, 5)$ or $(4, 8)$? Does your answer change under $U = \ln x + \ln y$?

35\. A consumer maximizes $U = xy$ with budget $2x + 4y = 80$. Use a Lagrange multiplier to find $x$, $y$, $U$ and $\lambda$, and say what $\lambda$ means.

36\. A firm must produce $Q = \sqrt{LK} = 6$. Labour costs \$4 and capital costs \$1 per unit. Find the cost-minimizing $L$ and $K$ and the minimum cost.

## More tools

37\. A project costs \$1000 now and pays \$400, \$500 and \$600 at the ends of years 1, 2 and 3. At a 10% discount rate, find the NPV and say whether to do it.

38\. A bond has face value \$1000, an annual coupon of 5% and two years to maturity. Find its price when the market yield is 4%, and say whether it sells at a premium or a discount.

39\. The marginal propensity to consume is 0.8. Find the spending multiplier, the change in output from a \$20 increase in government purchases and the change from a \$20 lump-sum tax cut.

40\. In a Keynesian cross, $C = 100 + 0.75Y$, $I = 150$ and $G = 250$. (a) Find equilibrium output. (b) Find it after $G$ rises to 290. (c) An oil price rise shifts short-run aggregate supply left. What happens to the price level and output?

41\. A regression of hourly wage on years of education gives $\widehat{\text{wage}} = 8 + 1.5\,\text{educ}$. Read the slope, predict the wage at 16 years and find the residual for a person with 16 years who earns \$35.

42\. (a) A log-log regression gives $\ln Q = 5 - 1.2\ln P$. Read the slope and find the exact change in $Q$ for a 10% rise in $P$. (b) The true model is $y = 2x + 3z$ and $z$ rises by 0.5 for each unit of $x$. If you regress $y$ on $x$ alone, what slope do you expect?

43\. Two firms choose a High or Low price. Payoffs (row, column) are $(H,H) = (5,5)$, $(H,L) = (1,6)$, $(L,H) = (6,1)$ and $(L,L) = (3,3)$. Find the Nash equilibrium and say whether there is a dominant strategy.

44\. Payoffs (row, column) are $(A,A) = (2,2)$, $(A,B) = (0,0)$, $(B,A) = (0,0)$ and $(B,B) = (1,1)$. Find all pure Nash equilibria and the mixed equilibrium.
