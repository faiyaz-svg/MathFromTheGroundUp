## Cheat sheet

### Variables and expressions

A variable is a name for a number, either an unknown or a quantity that varies. An expression names a number; an equation is a statement that is true or false.

| Part | Meaning | In $4x^2 - x + 7$ |
|---|---|---|
| term | a part that is added, with the sign in front of it | $4x^2$, $-x$, $7$ |
| coefficient | the number that multiplies the variable part | $4$ and $-1$ |
| constant term | a term with no variable | $7$ |
| like terms | same letters with the same exponents | $3x^2$ and $-5x^2$, but not $3x^2$ and $3x$ |

Implied multiplication: $3x = 3 \cdot x$, $3x^2 = 3 \cdot (x^2)$, $-x^2 = -(x^2)$ and $2(x + 1) = 2 \cdot (x + 1)$.

To evaluate, put every substituted value in brackets. At $x = -3$: $x^2 = (-3)^2 = 9$, $-x^2 = -9$ and $-x = 3$.

| Words | Expression |
|---|---|
| 4 more than $n$ | $n + 4$ |
| 4 less than $n$, 4 subtracted from $n$ | $n - 4$ |
| 5 less than twice $n$ | $2n - 5$ |
| twice the sum of $n$ and 5 | $2(n + 5)$ |
| the quotient of $n$ and 3 | $\frac{n}{3}$ |

Test a translation with a number such as 10: the words and the expression must give the same value.

### Equivalent expressions

Two expressions are equivalent when they have the same value for every value of the variable. An equation that is true for every value is an identity.

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| expand: $a(b + c) = ab + ac$ | $3(x + 4) = 3x + 12$ |
| combine like terms: $ax + bx = (a + b)x$ | $10x - 3x = 7x$ |
| minus before brackets: $-(a - b) = -a + b$ | $-(x - 3) = -x + 3$ |
| factor out the GCF | $18x^2 - 12x + 6 = 6(3x^2 - 2x + 1)$ |

To simplify: expand every bracket (the multiplier keeps its sign), group like terms, combine them, and write the highest power first. To factor: divide every term by the GCF and check by expanding.

To prove that two expressions are equivalent, rewrite one into the other and name the property behind each step. To prove that they are not, give one value where they differ. Agreeing at a few values proves nothing: $x^2 + 2$ and $3x$ agree at 1 and 2 but differ at 3. Test with values other than 0 and 1.

| Wrong | Right | Test |
|---|---|---|
| $-(x - 3) = -x - 3$ | $-x + 3$ | at $x = 0$: $3$, not $-3$ |
| $2(x + 3) = 2x + 3$ | $2x + 6$ | at $x = 4$: $14$, not $11$ |
| $3x + 2 = 5x$ | $3x + 2$ is already simplified | at $x = 2$: $8$, not $10$ |
| $\frac{x}{2} + \frac{x}{3} = \frac{2x}{5}$ | $\frac{5}{6}x$ | at $x = 6$: $5$, not $2.4$ |
| $2(xy) = (2x)(2y)$ | $2xy$ | at $x = 3$, $y = 5$: $30$, not $60$ |

### One- and two-step equations

A solution makes the equation true, and the solution set is the set of all solutions. Equivalent equations have the same solution set.

- Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides gives an equivalent equation.
- Multiplying or dividing both sides by the same nonzero number gives an equivalent equation. Multiplying by 0 is not allowed: it turns every equation into $0 = 0$.
- Undo the operations in reverse order. For $3x + 11 = 2$, subtract 11, then divide by 3: $x = -3$.
- For a fraction coefficient, multiply by the reciprocal: $\frac{2}{3}x = 10$ gives $x = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 10 = 15$.
- The equation $-x = 8$ is not solved yet: it means $x = -8$.
- Check in the original equation, never in a later line.

For a word problem, name the unknown with its units, write the two quantities that are equal as an equation, solve, and check the answer against the words.

### Multi-step equations

1. Clear fractions: multiply every term by the LCD.
2. Expand brackets.
3. Combine like terms on each side.
4. Gather the variable on the side with the larger coefficient, and the constants on the other side.
5. Divide by the coefficient.
6. Check in the original equation.

Every linear equation reduces to $ax = b$:

| Case | Solutions | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| $a \ne 0$ | exactly one, $x = \frac{b}{a}$ | $3x = 12$, so $x = 4$ |
| $a = 0$ and $b \ne 0$ | none; the solution set is $\emptyset$ | the $x$ terms cancel and leave $6 = 5$ |
| $a = 0$ and $b = 0$ | every number; an identity | the $x$ terms cancel and leave $0 = 0$ |

A result of $0 = 0$ means every number is a solution, not $x = 0$.

Divide both sides only by a number you know is not 0. Dividing $5x = 3x$ by $x$ gives the false $5 = 3$ and loses the solution $x = 0$; subtract $3x$ instead.

When you clear fractions, keep brackets around a numerator with two terms: $12 \cdot \frac{x - 1}{4} = 3(x - 1) = 3x - 3$, and $-12 \cdot \frac{x - 1}{4} = -3x + 3$.

### Inequalities

| Symbol | Read as | Graph of the boundary |
|---|---|---|
| $<$, $>$ | is less than, is greater than | open dot (left out) |
| $\le$, $\ge$ | is less than or equal to, is greater than or equal to | closed dot (included) |

The statement $a < b$ means $b - a$ is positive, so $a$ is to the left of $b$ on the number line.

Solve as for an equation, with one change: multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the symbol. The reason is that $2 < 3$ but $-2 > -3$. Adding or subtracting never reverses it, and a negative number elsewhere in the inequality doesn't either: $3x < -12$ gives $x < -4$.

| Words | Symbol |
|---|---|
| at least, no less than, a minimum of | $\ge$ |
| at most, no more than, a maximum of | $\le$ |
| more than, greater than, over | $>$ |
| less than, fewer than, under | $<$ |

- Write the answer with the variable on the left: $-15 > x$ is $x < -15$.
- Check twice: the boundary makes the two sides equal, and a test number from the shaded part makes the inequality true.
- When the variable counts whole things, round in the direction the inequality allows: $n \le 38.75$ means at most 38.
- A compound inequality such as $-2 < x \le 5$ means both parts hold. Solve $-3 \le 2x + 1 < 7$ by doing the same thing to all three parts: $-2 \le x < 3$.
- If the variable terms cancel, a false statement ($1 < -2$) means no solution and a true one ($2 \ge 0$) means every number.
