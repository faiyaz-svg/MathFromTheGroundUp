## Cheat sheet

### Ratios and unit rates

A ratio $a:b$ compares $a$ with $b$ by division, and for $b \ne 0$ its value is $\frac{a}{b}$. A rate compares quantities with different units, and a unit rate is the amount for one unit of the second.

| Task | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simplify a ratio | same units, clear decimals or fractions, then divide every term by the GCF | 45 min to 2 h is $45:120 = 3:8$ |
| Part-to-whole from part-to-part | the parts of $a:b$ are $\frac{a}{a+b}$ and $\frac{b}{a+b}$ of the whole | $3:5$ gives $\frac{3}{8}$ and $\frac{5}{8}$ |
| Split an amount in $a:b:c$ | one part is the amount divided by $a + b + c$; multiply it by each term | \$2,850 in $6:5:4$ is \$1,140, \$950 and \$760 |
| Unit rate | divide, with the "per" quantity on the bottom | \$342 for 18 h is \$19 per hour |
| Price per 100 g | divide the price by the mass in hundreds of grams | \$8.99 for 340 g is $8.99 \div 3.4$, about \$2.64 |
| Convert a rate's units | multiply by conversion factors so the unwanted units cancel | \$2.99/lb is $2.99 \div 0.45359237$, about \$6.59/kg |

- Multiplying or dividing every term by the same nonzero number gives an equivalent ratio. For $b, d \ne 0$, the ratios $a:b$ and $c:d$ are equivalent exactly when $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$.
- Flip a rate when it helps: 12.5 km/L is 0.08 L/km, which is 8 L/100 km.

Main mistakes:

- Writing the terms in the wrong order. Say the ratio in words first.
- Reading a part-to-part ratio as part-to-whole: in $3:5$, the first part is $\frac{3}{8}$ of the whole, not $\frac{3}{5}$.
- Adding the same number to every term: $1:3$ and $2:4$ are not equivalent.
- Comparing \$1.29/100 g with \$12.20/kg without converting. The second is \$1.22/100 g.
- Rounding each share separately: \$100 in $2:3:4$ rounds to \$99.99, so one share gets the extra cent.

### Proportional relationships

A proportion $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, with $b, d \ne 0$, is equivalent to $ad = bc$. Cross-multiplying is multiplying both sides by $bd$.

To solve one, keep the same quantity in the same place on both sides, cross-multiply and divide: $\frac{27}{360} = \frac{x}{500}$ gives $360x = 13{,}500$, so $x = 37.5$.

The quantities $x$ and $y$ are proportional when $y = kx$ for a nonzero constant $k$, the constant of proportionality. It is the unit rate, $k = \frac{y}{x}$.

| Given | Proportional when |
|---|---|
| a table | $y \div x$ is the same in every row, and $x = 0$ goes with $y = 0$ |
| a graph | the points lie on a straight line through the origin |
| an equation | it has the form $y = kx$, with nothing added on |

At a scale of $1:n$, a real length is $n$ times the drawing length. At $1:100{,}000$, 1 cm stands for 1 km. Areas scale by $n^2$ and volumes by $n^3$.

Main mistakes:

- Using a proportion when there is a fixed charge. A taxi at \$4.50 plus \$2 per km charges \$14.50 for 5 km and \$24.50 for 10 km, not \$29.00. Ask whether zero of one gives zero of the other.
- Flipping one side of the proportion, which mixes up units such as L/km and km/L.
- Reading $1:50{,}000$ as 1 cm to 50,000 m. Both sides use the same unit, so 1 cm stands for 500 m.
- Deciding from one pair of values. Check every row.

### Percents

The % sign means divide by 100: $x\% = \frac{x}{100}$, so $7\% = 0.07$ and $250\% = 2.5$. The main equation is $\text{part} = \text{percent} \times \text{whole}$, and the whole is the answer to "percent of what?", usually the noun after "of".

| Unknown | Compute | Example |
|---|---|---|
| the part | percent times whole | $15\%$ of 80 is $0.15 \times 80 = 12$ |
| the whole | part divided by percent | 12 is $15\%$ of $12 \div 0.15 = 80$ |
| the percent | part divided by whole | $12 \div 80 = 0.15 = 15\%$ |

- With 13% HST, the total is $1.13 \times \text{price}$, and the price is $\text{total} \div 1.13$.
- In your head: 10% moves the decimal point one place left, and 1% moves it two places. Build the rest from those: 5% is half of 10%, 15% is $10\% + 5\%$, and 13% is $10\% + 3 \times 1\%$.
- The two numbers can swap, since $x\%$ of $y$ equals $y\%$ of $x$: 8% of 50 is 50% of 8, which is 4.
- Worth memorizing: $\frac{1}{8} = 12.5\%$, $\frac{1}{5} = 20\%$, $\frac{1}{4} = 25\%$, $\frac{1}{3} \approx 33.3\%$, $\frac{2}{3} \approx 66.7\%$, $\frac{3}{4} = 75\%$.

Main mistakes:

- Using the wrong whole: in a seminar of 12 women and 18 men, the women are $12 \div 30 = 40\%$, not $12 \div 18$.
- Taking tax off by subtracting 13% of the total. Divide by 1.13 instead.
- Reading 0.5% as 0.5. It is 0.005.
- Rounding in the middle. Round once, at the end.

### Percent change and applications

A percent change is measured against the old value, which is the whole:

$$
\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%.
$$

With the rate $r$ written as a decimal, every change is a multiplication by its multiplier $1 + r$:

| Situation | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| increase by $r$ | multiply by $1 + r$ | a 7.5% rise is $\times 1.075$ |
| decrease by $r$ | multiply by $1 - r$ | 30% off is $\times 0.70$ |
| find the old value | divide the new value by $1 + r$ | $21.85 \div 1.15 = 19$ |
| several changes | multiply the multipliers | $1.10 \times 0.90 = 0.99$, a 1% loss |
| the same change $n$ times | multiply by $(1 + r)^n$ | $1.02^{10} \approx 1.219$, a 21.9% rise |
| stacked discounts, then HST | multiply all the multipliers | $120 \times 0.60 \times 0.75 \times 1.13 = 61.02$ |

- Percentage points measure the plain difference between two percents. A rate going from 4% to 5% rises 1 percentage point, which is a 25% increase. A basis point is a hundredth of a percentage point.
- Markup is profit divided by cost, and margin is profit divided by price. So $\text{price} = \text{cost} \times (1 + \text{markup}) = \text{cost} \div (1 - \text{margin})$, and a 50% markup is a 33.3% margin.
- Simple interest is $I = Prt$, with $t$ in years for an annual rate $r$. The finance lessons write the principal $P$ as $\text{PV}$.
- To undo a change, divide by its multiplier: a 25% rise is undone by a 20% fall, and a 50% fall needs a 100% rise.
- Small rates almost add: $1.02 \times 1.03 = 1.0506$, close to 5%.

Main mistakes:

- Dividing by the new value: \$1,299 to \$1,049 is a 19.2% decrease, not 23.8%.
- Undoing a change with the same percent: $1.25 \times 0.75 = 0.9375$, not 1.
- Adding stacked discounts: 40% off and then 25% off is 55% off, not 65%.
- Saying "1%" for 1 percentage point.
- Mixing up markup and margin.
- Calling a tripling a 300% increase. It is a 200% increase.
- Percent change from 0 is undefined.
