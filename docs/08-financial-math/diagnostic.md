# Financial math diagnostic

This quiz covers the 27 lessons of the financial math folder, with two questions on each, 54 questions in all. Take it without notes, and write every answer down before you look at the answers. A calculator is allowed, and you should use it where a question asks for a decimal. Most questions take one to three minutes, so plan on three or four sittings rather than one. Every question here is a small version of something its lesson teaches. Where a question needs a Canadian tax, savings or mortgage figure, it uses the 2026 value from the lessons and says "as of 2026". All names, prices, rates and balances in the questions are made up (hypothetical).

Mark each answer right, wrong or guessed. A guess counts as wrong, even when it turns out right. Then do only the lessons behind the questions you marked wrong or guessed; the answer table names the lesson for each one. Several questions ask for a reason or a yes-or-no choice, and an answer without its reason counts as wrong. Money answers should be right to the cent, so keep every digit of a rate until the last step.

## Everyday money

1\. A \$120 jacket is 30% off. You also have a \$15 coupon that comes off before tax, and Ontario HST is 13% (as of 2026). What do you pay at the till?

2\. A restaurant bill with HST comes to \$90.40. Find (a) the price before tax and (b) a 15% tip on the pre-tax amount.

3\. Juice A is 750 mL for \$3.99 and juice B is 2 L for \$9.49. Which has the lower price per 100 mL, and by how much?

4\. A box of cereal costs \$5.49 for 500 g. The price stays the same but the box shrinks to 440 g. By what percent does the unit price rise?

5\. A worker earns \$78,000 a year, paid biweekly. Find the gross pay per cheque, then the CPP and the EI taken from it, using the 2026 rates (CPP 5.95% above a \$3,500 yearly exemption, EI 1.63%) and ignoring the yearly maximums.

6\. A part-time worker earns \$24.50 an hour for 37.5 hours a week, 52 weeks a year. Find the yearly gross pay and the gross pay per biweekly cheque. Separately, a person has \$9,000 saved and \$2,400 of essential costs a month. How many months of costs does the savings cover?

## Interest and growth

7\. Find the simple interest on \$4,500 at 3.6% a year for 200 days, using days divided by 365.

8\. You borrow \$2,000 and repay \$2,150 after 9 months, all of the extra being simple interest. What yearly rate is that?

9\. Find the value of \$6,000 after 7 years at 4.2% a year compounded monthly.

10\. How many years does it take \$5,000 to grow to \$8,000 at 5% compounded annually?

11\. Find the effective annual rate of 6% compounded monthly. Then compare account A (4.9% compounded monthly) with account B (5.0% compounded semi-annually). Which pays more?

12\. A loan of \$2,000 for one year at 6% has a \$40 fee that is taken from the amount you receive. What is the cost as a yearly rate on the money you actually get?

13\. Find the value of \$2,000 after 6 years at 3.5% compounded continuously.

14\. An account pays an effective annual rate of 5%. What continuously compounded rate is equivalent?

15\. Use the rule of 72 to estimate the years for money to double at 9%. Then find the exact value with logarithms.

16\. How many years does it take money to triple at 7% a year? Which rule-of-thumb numerator estimates this, and what does it give?

17\. A savings account pays 6.5% and inflation is 3%. Find the exact real return, and compare it with the approximation nominal rate minus inflation.

18\. A salary stays at \$50,000 for 10 years while inflation runs at 2.5% a year. What is that salary worth in today's dollars at the end of the 10 years?

## Time value of money

19\. (a) Find the value of \$3,000 after 6 years at 4% a year. (b) Find the present value of \$15,000 due in 5 years at 4.8% compounded monthly.

20\. You can have \$1,000 now or \$1,100 in two years. At 4% a year, which is worth more, and what is the \$1,100 worth today?

21\. You save \$250 at the end of each month for 20 years at 6% compounded monthly. What is the balance at the end, and how much of it is your deposits?

22\. (a) What payment at the end of each year grows to \$50,000 in 8 years at 3% compounded annually? (b) What is a perpetuity of \$1,200 a year worth at 4%?

23\. Find the monthly payment on a \$24,000 loan over 5 years at 6.6% compounded monthly, and the total interest.

24\. A loan of \$18,000 at 7.2% compounded monthly over 60 months has a payment of \$358.12. Split the first payment into interest and principal, and give the balance after it.

## Borrowing

25\. An Ontario student loan of \$9,000 charges prime plus 1%, with prime at 5.0% (so 6%). Interest is added monthly during the six months after studies. Find the balance when repayment starts.

26\. Starting from that balance of \$9,273.40, find the Ontario payment over 120 months at 6%. A separate federal loan of \$15,000 has no interest (as of 2026) and is repaid over 120 months. What is its payment?

27\. A car costs \$32,000 before HST (13%, as of 2026). You pay \$4,000 down and finance the rest at 6.0% compounded monthly over 60 months. Find the amount financed, the payment and the interest paid.

28\. For the loan in question 27, stretching the term to 84 months changes the payment and the total interest to what? Separately, a \$32,000 car loses 14% of its value each year. What is it worth after 4 years?

29\. A car has a net price of \$30,000, a residual value of \$18,000, a 36-month lease and a rate of 6%. With the money factor $m = \dfrac{6}{2400}$, find the monthly payment before tax and with 13% HST (as of 2026).

30\. A lessor quotes a money factor of 0.0018. What yearly rate is that? Does a higher residual value raise or lower the payment, and why?

31\. A credit card balance of \$2,400 is at 18%, using the monthly-rate model. You pay \$120 a month and make no new purchases. Find the first month's interest and the number of months to pay it off.

32\. The minimum payment on the same \$2,400 balance at 18% is 3% of the balance. What is the balance after one month? True or false, with a reason: a cash advance has the same grace period as a purchase.

33\. A card has an \$8,000 limit and a \$2,800 balance. Find the utilization, and the balance that brings it to 30%. True or false: the formula behind credit scores is public.

34\. You owe \$2,000 at 22% and \$6,000 at 8%. Find the blended rate. When you have extra money, which debt gets it under the avalanche method, and why?

## Housing

35\. A homeowner's monthly costs are mortgage interest \$1,900, mortgage principal \$1,100, property tax \$400, maintenance \$300 and home insurance \$80. How much of this is unrecoverable? Also find the price-to-rent ratio for a \$600,000 home that rents for \$2,400 a month.

37\. For a \$750,000 home, find the minimum down payment (as of 2026). Then, with that down payment, find the loan-to-value ratio, the mortgage insurance premium, and the total loan after the premium is added.

39\. A \$400,000 mortgage has a quoted rate of 4.8%, monthly payments and a 25-year amortization. Use the Canadian rule for fixed-rate mortgages to find the payment, then split the first payment into interest and principal and give the balance after it.

40\. For the same mortgage, find the balance after 5 years (60 payments) using the closed form, and the total interest over the 25 years.

41\. A fixed mortgage is quoted at 4.8% with monthly payments. Find the rate per payment and the effective annual rate. Why is dividing 4.8% by 12 wrong?

42\. Offer A is a 5.0% mortgage compounded semi-annually. Offer B is 4.95% compounded monthly. Which has the lower effective annual rate?

## Investing and retirement

43\. You buy a fund for \$2,000. Three years later it is worth \$2,300 and it paid \$30 of income along the way. Find the holding-period return and the annualized return.

44\. Three yearly returns are $+25\%$, $-20\%$ and $+10\%$. Find the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean. Then find the gain needed after a 40% loss to break even.

45\. A sum of \$25,000 earns a gross 6.5% a year for 20 years. Compare the final balance with a 0.25% MER against a 1.90% MER. What share of the final balance does the higher fee cost?

46\. You invest \$100 each month in a fund priced at \$20, \$25 and \$40 in three months. How many shares do you buy, what is your average cost per share, and how does it compare with the average price?

47\. You put \$6,000 of pre-tax income into an RRSP, taxed at 30% now and 20% when you withdraw. A TFSA gets the \$4,200 you keep after tax now. Both grow at 5% a year for 20 years. Compare the after-tax amounts.

48\. Using the 2026 rules: (a) what is the RRSP room from earned income of \$90,000 last year? (b) You opened an FHSA last year and put nothing in it. How much can you contribute this year? (c) When is a TFSA withdrawal made in March 2026 added back to your room?

## Taxes, insurance and planning

49\. Consider federal tax only, before credits, with the 2026 slices of 14% up to \$58,523 and 20.5% from \$58,523 to \$117,045. Find the tax on taxable income of \$80,000, the effective rate, and the extra tax on a \$2,000 raise.

50\. At \$65,000 the combined Ontario marginal rate is 29.65% (as of 2026). How much does a \$3,000 RRSP deduction save? A friend says a raise into a higher bracket can cut your take-home pay. True or false, with a reason?

51\. A policy covers a 4% chance of a \$2,500 loss and a 1% chance of a \$10,000 loss, and it costs \$260. Find the expected loss, the loading and the loss ratio.

52\. (a) A warranty costs \$240 and covers an \$800 repair that has a 20% chance of happening. Find the expected repair cost and the chance that makes the warranty break even. (b) How much term life cover replaces 10 years of \$40,000 income, discounted at 3%?

53\. Write the spreadsheet formula for the monthly payment on \$20,000 at 6% a year over 48 months, and give what it returns. Why is the value negative?

54\. Cash flows are $-1000$ today and 400, 500 and 600 at the end of years 1 to 3. Write the formula for the NPV at 8% and give its value. Is the IRR above or below 8%? Also give the value of `=FV(0.04/12,60,-200)`.
